Automated cognitive tests

Jan 2025 – Sep 2025 · With Hei Ching Iu · Prof. Xiaojuan Ma

The grocery-shopping task is a four-part cognitive screening dialogue. The participant’s answers are part of the measurement. A conversational agent can administer the script: speech is transcribed, and the model replies from the task rules and from the figurine’s position on the shop map. Response quality depends on the prompt structure and on the accuracy of the transcript.

Hu et al. showed that a three-step scaffold improves this dialogue: classify the participant’s intention, match a strategy for that step, then select the reply. Hei Ching Iu and I ablated that scaffold on GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct. I ran and labelled the two intermediate prompts. Hei Ching Iu ran the full scaffold and the minimal prompt, and labelled turns that contained speech-recognition errors. I wrote the results, discussion, and conclusion of the group report.

Prompt conditions

The four prompts ablate the original scaffold. The first retains the three steps and passes each step’s answer, together with its reason, to the next step. The second collapses the three steps into one question and still requests a reason. The third uses that question and omits the reason. The fourth states only the situation and the task rules. The comparison separates the staged handoff, the written rationale, and the task rules.

Evaluation

We drew three dialogues at random from 32 user studies: 65, 57, and 43 turns, of which 15, 11, and 18 contained a speech-recognition error. Every turn was run under all four prompts and both models. Each reply was labelled appropriate or inappropriate by hand. We then scored three partitions of the same turns: transcripts without recognition errors, transcripts with recognition errors, and the mixed input a deployed system would receive. The reported rate is the share of appropriate replies, averaged across the three dialogues.

The full scaffold outperforms the other three conditions, particularly on error-free transcripts. Requesting a written reason does not raise the rate above the same single question without that request, and the minimal instruction falls in the same lower range. This result is contrary to the expectation that an explicit rationale would improve responses on its own. Recognition errors lower every condition. As the prompt becomes less structured, Qwen2.5 outperforms GPT-4o. On turns with recognition errors, its two less structured prompts remain the higher of the two models. About 53% of turns in each dialogue are navigation commands, and Qwen2.5 answers those commands consistently even when the transcript is inaccurate.

A less structured prompt can still produce a plausible sentence on a noisy turn. Without the three-step scaffold, both models more often fail to change tasks. A short prompt also sometimes answers a simple request incorrectly, while the staged prompt handles the same request consistently. An appropriate-looking reply can therefore correspond to the wrong step of the task.

Limitations

The labels record whether a reply was appropriate. They do not measure whether the model identified the current step of the task. Three dialogues also cover only part of the interaction space in the 32 studies. A follow-up would score whether the guidance was the correct next action, separately from the surface plausibility of the sentence, and would use more of the collected dialogues.
