Newcomers on RedNote

Jan 2025 – Sep 2025 · With Kangyu Yuan, Hanfang Lyu, Ziqi Pan, Yuanhao Zhang, Junze Li, Bingcan Guo, Jiaxiong Hu, and Qingyu Guo · Prof. Xiaojuan Ma

In January 2025 the U.S. government moved to ban TikTok, and a large wave of its users arrived on RedNote, a platform whose existing community is mostly Chinese. They called themselves TikTok refugees. For a short time, people who do not usually share a social network were talking to each other in public.

In the HKUST HCI Initiative we studied how that arrival went. The material was about 3,510 RedNote posts, mainly from the United States (about 47%) and China (about 39%). We read them with open coding, labeling what the posts were doing without a fixed list in advance. That pass produced twelve strategies newcomers used in the cross-cultural conversation.

Two observations mattered for design. Posts that were friendly, and easy to understand, carried much of the success of the first contact. Staying in the conversation was a different problem: a warm hello does not by itself become a habit of coming back. The paper discusses what a platform can do to support that kind of meeting.

The full author list is on Publications. The paper is in the CHI 2025 extended abstracts. Paper.
