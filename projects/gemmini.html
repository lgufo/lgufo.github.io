Hardware–software co-optimization on Gemmini

Spring 2026 · ELEC 5140 Advanced Computer Architecture · with Puyang Zhang

DNN inference is dominated by multiply-accumulates and by the data movement those operations require. Gemmini generates a systolic-array accelerator attached to a RISC-V core. The core issues commands. The array performs the arithmetic, passing operands between neighboring processing elements. A scratchpad and an accumulator hold the current tile on chip. A DMA engine moves tiles to and from main memory, and a TLB translates the addresses.

Puyang Zhang and I evaluated MLP and CNN kernels on Gemmini’s simulator. I was responsible for the hardware parameter sweep: systolic-array size (8×8, 16×16, and 32×32), on-chip memory, the TLB, the maximum number of in-flight memory requests, and the reservation station, which queues loads, stores, and executes. Puyang Zhang was responsible for manual loop tiling, choosing tiles that fit the scratchpad and the accumulator. The reported speedups come from changing both.

Small MLP and CNN workloads

The small MLP is two matrix multiplications, 16×64×128 followed by 16×128×32. The small CNN maps a 13×13 input with 3 channels to a 7×7 output with 16 channels. A larger array reduces cycle count when the kernel is compute-bound. On the small CNN the reduction is small: moving from 8×8 to 16×16 lowers the total from 5,129 cycles to 4,827, while TLB wait remains near 1,850 cycles and reservation-station-full cycles remain near 4,600.

Manual tiles sized to the array and the on-chip memories change the totals by more. On the 16×16 MLP, cycles fall from 3,985 to 2,207, a 1.8× speedup. On the CNN with a larger TLB and a higher in-flight limit, they fall from 4,530 to 2,728, a 1.66× speedup. In the figure, “+TLB” denotes that TLB and in-flight configuration.

The counter breakdown accounts for the difference. On the MLP, execute cycles fall from 4,278 to 1,462 when the array grows from 8×8 to 16×16, and that reduction is most of the change in the total. On the CNN, execute cycles are already 1,072 at 8×8. TLB wait stays near 1,850 cycles, and reservation-station-full cycles stay near 4,600. The additional array width has little arithmetic to absorb, and the stalls remain.

Large CNN workload

The larger CNN has a 28×28 output, 128 input channels, 128 output channels, and a 3×3 kernel. After im2col, the multiplication is 784×1152 by 1152×128. The workload is compute-heavy, and the full output does not fit in the accumulator as one tile. Accumulator rows used by a tile are ceil(output channels / array width) × output rows × output columns. The schedule keeps that quantity under half of the accumulator rows, leaving room for double buffering. On the 32×32 array the budget is 512 rows. A full output tile requires 3,136 rows. The manual tile, 18 output rows by 28 output columns by 32 output channels, uses 504 rows.

Under automatic tiling, increasing the array from 16×16 to 32×32 reduces cycles from 523,798 to 228,781, about 2.3×. Manual tiles on the 32×32 array reduce that further to 130,877, about 1.7× relative to automatic tiling on the same array. Relative to the 16×16 automatic baseline, the combined speedup is 4.0×. The same manual tiles reduce the 16×16 total only from 523,798 to 468,703. Tile shape therefore matters more once the array is wide enough to stall for lack of work.

Cycle breakdown on the 32×32 array

On the 32×32 array, execute cycles are about 111,000 under both schedules. The difference is in the stalls. TLB wait falls from 86,842 cycles to 1,646. Reservation-station-full cycles fall from 219,468 to 121,554. The manual tile does not increase arithmetic throughput. It reduces stalls on address translation and on a full reservation station.

TLB and reservation-station sweeps

On the same convolution, a larger TLB and a higher in-flight limit do not improve the result by themselves. With automatic tiling, configurations of 4 TLB entries with 16 in-flight requests and 8 entries with 32 in-flight requests both take 523,798 cycles. Raising the pair to 16 entries and 64 in-flight requests increases the total to 887,324, with both execute cycles and reservation-station-full cycles higher. Manual tiling stays near 469,000 cycles at all three settings. The larger memory configuration perturbs the automatic schedule. The manual tile, which already fits the accumulator, is stable across the same sweep.

The reservation-station sweep has the same structure. Entries are listed as load, store, and execute. A 4/4/4 configuration takes 889,169 cycles with automatic tiles and 800,332 with manual tiles. Raising only the execute entries, to 4/4/8, yields 523,798 and 469,548. The default 8/4/16 yields 523,798 and 468,703. For this convolution, the execute queue determines the total. Load entries beyond 4 and execute entries beyond 8 leave it unchanged. On the small CNN, a larger TLB does reduce automatic-tiling cycles, from 4,827 to 4,530, because that kernel is stalled on address translation.
