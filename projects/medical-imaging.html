Medical imaging experiments

Aug 2024 – Dec 2024 · With Prof. Xiaomeng Li and Ph.D. student Haonan Wang

This was my first research project. I learned the ordinary training loop in PyTorch — load a batch, run the model, compute a loss, step the optimizer — and used it on two medical tasks.

Benign or malignant, from a skin photo

The first task was to tell a harmless skin lesion from one that can spread, using the ISIC 2016 collection: 900 training images and 379 test images. I started from ResNet-50, a vision model already trained on ordinary photographs, and replaced its last layer with a single yes-or-no score. Training used Adam and binary cross-entropy. The two classes are uneven, so a weighted sampler drew the rarer class more often and kept the model from learning only the common one. A score above one half counted as malignant. Accuracy landed near 90% on the training images and about 80% on the test images.

Where is the spleen, in a CT volume

The second task asks for a map, not a single label: which voxels in a 3D CT scan belong to the spleen. I used a U-Net. It squeezes the volume down until the organ is easy to recognize, then expands back to a mask the same size as the input. The scans arrive in different shapes, so they were resized to a shared one. Training again reached about 90% on the training set and about 80% on the test set.

Both were a first pass at taking a known architecture and fitting it to a medical dataset. Along the way I learned the training loop properly, and how to run it on a remote GPU.
